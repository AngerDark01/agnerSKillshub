# Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework

Apply Christensen's "Competing Against Luck" framework to understand the real progress customers seek when they hire a product.

## Core Principle

Customers don't buy products — they hire them to make progress in their lives. The unit of analysis is the **job**, not the customer segment or product feature.

## Job Statement Format

```
When [circumstances / situation],
I want to [progress / motion toward goal],
so I can [desired outcome / final goal].
```

**Example:**
> When I'm commuting and can't read, I want to consume a book, so I can feel productive and arrive informed.

Write job statements for every significant use case before proposing solutions.

## Forces of Progress

Four forces govern whether a customer switches to a new solution or stays with the old one:

| Force | Direction | Description |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Push** | → Switch | Dissatisfaction / struggle with current solution |
| **Pull** | → Switch | Attraction to the new solution's promise |
| **Anxiety** | ← Stay | Fear of the unknown / switching risk |
| **Habit** | ← Stay | "I know this works" inertia |

Map all four forces before designing a solution. A product wins only when Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit.

## Big Hire vs. Little Hire

- **Big Hire**: Customer purchases / adopts (first use)
- **Little Hire**: Customer uses it again (repeated use)

Both must satisfy the job. Winning the Big Hire but losing the Little Hire (churn) means the product doesn't actually do the job.

## Non-Obvious Competition

The real competitor is whatever the customer uses *today* to make progress — not just direct product alternatives.

**Discovery question:** "What were you doing before you started using [product]? What did you do the last time you needed [outcome] but didn't use [product]?"

**Example:** Milkshake's real competition wasn't other milkshakes — it was bananas, bagels, and boredom.

## Discovery Interview Protocol

Conduct **switch interviews** — talk to recent switchers (hired or fired your product):

1. **Trigger scene**: "Walk me through the first time you thought you needed a change."
2. **Passive looking**: "What did you consider? How did you search?"
3. **Active looking**: "What tipped you into actually trying something new?"
4. **Deciding moment**: "What made you choose [product]?"
5. **First use**: "What happened the first time you used it? How did you feel?"
6. **Ongoing use**: "When does it work? When does it fail?"

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- Asking hypothetical questions ("Would you use...?")
- Leading with features ("What do you think of our X feature?")
- Aggregating answers — context is lost in averages

## Deliverables

For each opportunity being investigated:

- [ ] **Job Statement** — circumstance + motion + outcome
- [ ] **Forces Map** — Push/Pull/Anxiety/Habit with specific customer quotes
- [ ] **Hire/Fire story** — narrative from switch interview
- [ ] **Non-obvious competitor list** — what customers hire instead
- [ ] **Big/Little Hire success criteria** — how will you know the job is done?
